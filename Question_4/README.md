
High-throughput sexing of Cannabis sativa using REM16

Marker selection
In RNA-seq data from 138 plants (Task 3), REM16 expression was higher in females than males, with no overlap (p < 2.2e-16). FT1 showed no sex difference (p = 0.69). REM16 is therefore selected over FT1.

Workflow for collecting and processing samples
Firstly, fresh leaf samples (10–20 mg punch) will be collected matching the RNA-seq tissue and stage. Samples will be put quickly into barcoded 96-well plates in liquid nitrogen and named for plant lines. Stored samples (−80°C) will be crushed with steel beads in a TissueLyser. All materials must be clean, as contamination affects RNA quality. RNA will be extracted on a KingFisher Flex with a magnetic-bead kit (e.g., MagMAX Plant RNA) and DNase.

Sex determination
RNA (5 ng) will be used for one-step RT-qPCR with REM16 and the reference gene EF1α, validated as stable in Cannabis leaves (Guo et al., 2018), in triplicate. ΔCt = Ct(REM16) − Ct(EF1α). As the expected difference is ~1.8 cycles, a ΔCt threshold will be calibrated on plants of known sex. Low ΔCt indicates female; high ΔCt, male. As a complementary check, DNA will be isolated separately and PACE genotyping with CSP-2 primers (Toth et al., 2020), including known XX and XY controls, will verify REM16 results. Each qPCR plate includes known XX and XY, no-template and no-RT controls.

Scaling
96/384-well plates, KingFisher extraction, liquid-handling robots and automated Ct analysis allow hundreds of plants daily.
